Beginning with max_ray_depth = 0, only the light source is visible since all surfaces need at least one bounce.

With max_ray_depth = 1, the balls are dark. This is the effect of direct lighting. The spheres do reflect and refract the light source however.

With max_ray_depth = 2, the ball on the left, the ball with a mirror surface, is apparent. The ball on the right, the glass surface one, is still mainly dark. This is due to the fact that for a mirror, two bounces are used, one for the light to hit the ball and another to hit the box.

Part 2. Microfacet Material

All pictures have 128 samples per pixel, 1 sample per light, and 5 bounces

Alpha is the roughness parameter that controls how "bumpy" the microscopic surface is, and that affects how light is reflected. An α that is smaller appers more glossy and an α that is larger leads to less reflective and more matte. Lower α results in microfacets that are aligned with the surface normal. This leads to a strong reflection. As seen in the α = 0.005 rendered image, the dragon is more reflective than the other α values. At larger α, the angles of the microfacets are less aligned with the surface normal, and this leads to incoming rays to have a higher variance of outgoing angles. Thus, the surface looks more diffused and less glossy. As seen in the α = 0.5 dragon, which is quite matte and not reflective.


Cosine hemisphere sampling gives a noiser rendered image of the surface of the material. This is due to the fact that it samples uniformly, which may be in directions that do not form the correct mirrored angle. Importance sampling leads to a less noisy image of the surface of the material, with less dark spots, because it importance sampling biases samples towards ones with half vectors that align close with the surface normal.


I used η values of 0.33228, 1.0162, 1.2474, with corresponding k values of 3.1646, 2.5785, 2.4603 to get the material Indium.


Part 3. Environment Light

Environment lighting uses an environment map to simulate light arriving from all directions, like it is from a source that is infinitely far away. The light rays are essentially parallel, which is a good approximation for natural light from the sky, since the sun is a light source that is far away! By sampling this environment map, I am able to capture overall lighting, reflections, and shadows in a scene, to create more realistic renderings!

Part 4. Depth of Field

A pinhole camera has an infinitely small aperture, which means each light ray passes through a single point. This results in a rendered scene where all parts are in focus. On the other hand, a thin-lens camera model has a finite-sized lens, which creates a blurred effect. Only the objects that are lying on the plane that's focal distance away from the camera are in focus and those away from the plane appear out of focus due to the depth-of-field effects!

All pictures have a lens radius of 0.23.

As focal distance increases, the focus plane shifts from the edges of the box closest to the camera all the way to the wall of the box furthest away from the camera. In the middle, the half of the dragon closest to and furthest from the camera shift into focus.


All pictures have a focal depth of 4.5.

When aperture is 0, the entire scene is focus, since this is the same as a pinhole camera. As aperture increases, rays originating from a point on the scene enter the camera from a broader range of positions on the lens. For objects not the focus distance, the rays converge at different points, which leads to more blur. The picture with the highest aperture has the most blur, which makes sense.
